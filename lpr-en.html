The Problem
What Metrics Don't (Yet) Reveal
Leadership culture is shaped at the top. How much of it survives the journey through the organisation is something few companies can answer — because no one measures it.
01
The Transmission Fails
Whether strategic direction from C-Level reaches C-1 and C-2 — consistently, understood, and lived — remains structurally unresolved in most organisations.
02
Lagging Indicators Mislead
Attrition, productivity loss, and decision-making weakness in middle management are symptoms. By the time they become measurable, the underlying leadership issue has usually taken hold.
03
A Structural Measurement Gap
Employee surveys measure sentiment. Assessments measure potential. No standard instrument measures how effectively leadership actually flows through all levels of an organisation.
The 5 Dimensions
What the LPR Measures
01 -- COHERENCE
Strategic Consistency
Is the strategic leadership vision interpreted consistently across all levels -- or does each level adapt it to its own understanding?
02 -- AGILITY
Responsiveness
Does the leadership team respond to market shifts and external change with decisiveness -- or with institutional inertia?
03 -- TALENT IMPACT
Talent Attraction
Does middle management attract and develop high performers -- or lose them quietly to the competition?
04 -- DECISION POWER
Decision Quality
Are decisions made at the right level -- or do they migrate upward because accountability is not genuinely lived?
05 -- ACCOUNTABILITY CULTURE
Accountability
Is accountability a shared standard -- or an expectation that applies to everyone else?

Relevance
When Is the LPR Relevant?
SITUATION 01
Performance and potential are diverging
Results fall short of expectations. The leadership team is formally in place -- but the impact is missing. The LPR delivers the structured assessment that no internal conversation can replace.
SITUATION 02
Ahead of a strategic transformation
Restructuring, expansion, cultural change: whether the leadership architecture can carry what is strategically planned is not decided in the boardroom -- but two levels below it.
SITUATION 03
Following a leadership transition
A new CEO, a new C-Level. How deeply is the leadership culture embedded -- or does it depend on individuals? The LPR provides clarity before the question becomes costly.
SITUATION 04
As a strategic management instrument
Leadership quality not as a soft factor, but as a measurable management variable. Organisations that understand this use the LPR not once -- but periodically.
The Framework
Leadership Quality as a Continuous Management Variable
Leadership quality shifts -- with market pressure, with personnel changes, with strategic pivots. The LPR makes that shift visible. Periodically. Comparably. With clear implications for action.
01 -- INITIAL ASSESSMENT
The Baseline
Structured individual interviews and questionnaires for all participants. Signium analyses: where does leadership work -- and where does it not. The result is the Leadership Dashboard: a clear picture of the status quo, by level, dimension and priority.
02 -- ANNUAL LEADERSHIP REVIEW
The Development
A systematic comparison with the initial assessment: what has changed? Where was action taken -- and to what effect? Where are new risks emerging? Leadership quality becomes a measurable, manageable variable within the management system.
